Research framework

From awareness to circular action

The study connects Sustainable Consumption, Circular Economy principles and Behavioural Economics to examine the determinants of responsible consumption among Bengaluru youth.

01 · Problem

Awareness does not guarantee action

Environmental knowledge may coexist with inconsistent everyday choices. This creates the central attitude–behaviour problem examined by the study.

02 · Explanation

Behaviour is shaped by more than knowledge

Economic considerations, social influence, convenience, perceived control and willingness can affect whether sustainable intentions become repeated behaviour.

03 · Model

Target the point of friction

A Behavioural Circular Economy Model can use the observed pattern to identify where information, incentives, choice architecture and practical access may be most useful.

Behavioural Economics

Intention–action friction

People can value sustainability while still choosing options that are easier, cheaper, familiar or socially reinforced. The gap therefore points to a behavioural mechanism worth investigating.

Circular Economy

Consumption as a system

Responsible consumption extends beyond purchase decisions to use, maintenance, reuse, production choices and intervention design across the product life cycle.

SDG 12

Responsible consumption and production

The research aligns with SDG 12 by asking what behavioural conditions can support more sustainable patterns of consumption and production.

Research caution: The dashboard describes patterns in the current scored sample. These results should be interpreted as evidence for the study's behavioural model, not as causal proof or a population-wide estimate without further statistical validation.
Core project outcome

Behavioural Circular Economy Model

The model converts the research evidence into a practical pathway: identify the point of friction, match the intervention, and connect behaviour change to a circular outcome.

01

Awareness & Attitude

Knowledge, beliefs and willingness to act sustainably.

02

Influencing Factors

Economic, social, behavioural and contextual conditions.

03

Behavioural Barrier

The friction that prevents intention becoming consistent action.

04

Targeted Intervention

A response selected for the barrier rather than awareness alone.

05

Sustainable Behaviour

More consistent responsible choices in everyday consumption.

06

Circular Outcome

Reuse, reduced waste, resource efficiency and responsible consumption.

Model principle: the appropriate intervention depends on the behavioural barrier revealed by the evidence. Awareness is a starting point; the goal is to make sustainable action easier to adopt and repeat.
